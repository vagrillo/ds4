Reading

  1. The decay direction looks right. A (decay on) is never below B or C on any suite and takes 3 extra correct answers over 78 samples. Without decay (C), expansion sits slightly below native (B) on MMLU — consistent with full-strength experts beyond rank 8 adding noise the router never trained for, while decaying them keeps the benefit without the damage.
  2. No gap is statistically significant. 1–2 questions per suite at n=32/30/16: the honest summary is “no measurable quality cost, weak positive signal for the decay”. A formal ranking needs hundreds of samples per suite.
  3. The configuration has a real cost: ~15.5 experts/token decode at 18.5 t/s vs 22.9 t/s native (−19%). Expansion buys at best a marginal quality edge at a fifth of speed; its natural regime is quality-critical single shots, not throughput.
Small-sample caveat: identical questions across runs and deterministic sampling (temperature 0) make the relative comparison fair, but the sample counts are too small to rank configurations reliably.
